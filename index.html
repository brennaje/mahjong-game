How to play

You play your own hand against three automatic opponents. Bot settings let you show or hide their concealed tiles and control spoken bot-move narration; exposed melds remain visible. The narration describes draws, discards, claims, and turns skipped by claims. While speech plays, the active player's panel and the tile being named glow. Draws animate from the wall as they are spoken, and discards move to the center when narrated. Use Speak turn narration to turn all speech off. When tutor guidance is enabled, it suggests a discard based on pairs and sequence connections; treat it as a learning aid, not a guaranteed best move. When your turn requires a draw, click Draw, then click a tile to discard; use Draw for replacement tiles after a Kong as well. Drag tiles or use Alt+Left/Right while focused to arrange your hand. Click Declare win when the hand is complete. You can also choose any eligible claim after an opponent discards. After a hand ends, select Game review to compare your discards and win declarations with approximate self-draw estimates; meld claims, passes, Riichi, and Kong choices are not scored.

Choose a ruleset above; it applies to the next hand. Chinese Classical and Hong Kong use four melds and a pair. Riichi adds a basic riichi declaration and a small set of yaku. Taiwanese uses 16 tiles and five melds. American is an explicitly simplified, card-free practice mode using four matching sets, a natural pair, and jokers; it is not an official NMJL hand card. The scoreboard adds 1 point per win and carries totals across hands (until the page is reloaded); official hand scoring, Charleston, furiten, and special hands are not implemented.

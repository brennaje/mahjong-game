American card and target

Your saved cards stay in this browser. The pattern to win is shown in the center of the American table. Any card pattern wins for every player; the first to complete one wins. Best for my hand (auto) picks the pattern your dealt tiles are most likely to complete (fewest missing tiles, favoring tiles that are still available) and rechecks it after every Charleston pass, saying at the end whether it changed; the center shows the closest allowed suit/run variation and matched count. Solid tiles are your actual matching tiles and exposures, including jokers and flowers; faded tiles are still needed (jokers may fill sets of three or more, never singles or pairs). Consecutive numbers in a pattern name are a template: 1111 234 5555 may appear as 5555 678 9999. A named target is your own focus for the tutor; with Default you aim for your closest pattern. Bots always aim for their own closest pattern. Hand type applies to all four players from the next deal. Exposed (X) plays the X patterns: you may call discards to complete any group, called groups sit face up, and other players can swap jokers out of them. Concealed (C) plays the C patterns: you may call only your winning 14th tile, every tile stays hidden, and no one can swap your jokers. If a card has no C patterns, its X patterns are played concealed for +10 points.

Each new hand, the deal, and the end of the Charleston automatically select Best for my hand (auto), replacing any saved named target. During play, a better estimated pattern is offered beside your play controls: choose Change target or Keep my target. Your target and tutor focus will not change without your approval. Keeping a target dismisses that suggestion until your hand changes.

After passing a discard claim, choose Draw. If you are next and no claims remain, you draw immediately. Otherwise Draw queued shows while other players finish; you draw automatically on your next legal turn. A new claim decision for you cancels the queued draw. Passing never grants an extra draw, and another player's successful claim may skip your turn.

Edit, copy, import, or export cards

Format: name | points | X or C | regular or singles | groups | optional suit rule. X means exposed, C concealed; use singles for Singles and Pairs. Groups are tile:count, such as F:3 x2:2 x4:2 x6:3 x8:4. Lines starting with # are notes and are not played.

Fixed tiles: C1–C9 Craks, D1–D9 Dots, B1–B9 Bams, E S W N winds, R G dragons, 0 Soap, F flower. Suit letters x y z v mean "any suit" (each letter is one suit); Dx is the dragon matching suit x; D* any dragon; W* any wind; xn xn+1 xn+2 are consecutive numbers. Different letters are different suits unless you add suits 2 or suits 1-2 to set how many different suits the letters use. A group holds 1–6 identical tiles; enter naturals, not jokers.

How to play

Choose iPhone or PC layout above. In iPhone layout, use the display buttons to reveal extra information. After your discard or claim pass, choose Undo to change it, Continue after discarding, or Draw after passing a claim. Draw waits for your next legal turn if other players must act first. Other supported actions can be undone until your next action. Charleston passes cannot be undone because all four players pass simultaneously.

Your tiles automatically arrange toward your target at the deal and when your hand or target changes, including draws and Charleston passes. Card hands follow the target's group order, with jokers in eligible groups; other hands follow the tutor's sets-and-pair plan. Unused tiles go at the end. You can still drag to adjust the order between these changes.

You play your own hand against three automatic opponents. Bot settings let you show or hide their concealed tiles and control spoken bot-move narration; exposed melds remain visible. The narration describes draws, discards, claims, and turns skipped by claims. While speech plays, the active player's panel and the tile being named glow. Draws animate from the wall as they are spoken, and discards move to the center when narrated. Use Speak turn narration to turn all speech off. When tutor guidance is enabled, it suggests a discard based on pairs and sequence connections; treat it as a learning aid, not a guaranteed best move. When your turn requires a draw, click Draw, then click a tile to discard. In the non-American rulesets, also use Draw for replacement tiles after a Kong; in American play, discard after a Kong claim. Drag tiles or use Alt+Left/Right while focused to arrange your hand. Click Declare win when the hand is complete. You can also choose any eligible claim after an opponent discards. After a hand ends, select Game review to compare your discards and win declarations with approximate self-draw estimates; meld claims, passes, Riichi, and Kong choices are not scored.

Choose a ruleset above; it applies to the next hand. Chinese Classical and Hong Kong use four melds and a pair. Riichi adds a basic riichi declaration and a small set of yaku. Taiwanese uses 16 tiles and five melds. American uses Craks, Dots, Bams, winds, dragons, flowers, and jokers; blank tiles are not in play. Four two-high walls are shown before dealing. On a new game, each player rolls two dice; tied high rolls are repeated to choose East. East rolls again to break the wall: count that many stacks from East's right and begin dealing at the highlighted stack. Select Deal tiles to start the Charleston. East rotates on the next hand. Choose a saved card, hand type (Exposed X or Concealed C), and target pattern in the American card panel (shown only for American), or use simplified card-free practice; the target pattern is shown in the center of the table with its tile images. Best for my hand (auto) picks the pattern your tiles are most likely to complete after the deal and rechecks it after every Charleston pass. Any card pattern wins for every player; a target only sets your own focus. Every other ruleset also shows its pattern to win in the center, and the bots play toward their own closest pattern or card line. In American, Bot test lets bots play all four seats until one wins. Select three non-jokers for each pass; on the last pass of either Charleston you can select fewer and blind pass the remaining tiles. After the first Charleston you can choose whether to continue; the bots always agree. Scores carry across hands until the page is reloaded; the simplified and card-based American scores are separate. Furiten and some special hands in other rulesets are not implemented.

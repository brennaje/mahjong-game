# Mahjong Game

A static browser game. No build step or package installation is required.

Your seat and controls stay at the bottom of the table; bot seats stay fixed while East and the other winds rotate between hands.

## Layout and undo

Choose **iPhone** or **PC** in the Layout menu. Drag your tiles with a mouse or finger to rearrange them at any time, in every ruleset, including during the Charleston and other players' turns; a focused tile can also be moved with Alt+Left/Right. A tap or click without dragging still selects or discards as usual. The iPhone view keeps the seats, recent discards, your tiles, turn status, and actions visible. Turn the phone sideways to use the wider landscape layout; tiles and text stay upright. Allow screen rotation in the phone's settings. Tiles shrink to the number in each hand so every hand fits across the screen in either orientation, even with bot tiles shown: the top bot uses one row, side bots use three rows in portrait and two in landscape, and your hand uses two rows in portrait and one in landscape. Expanded panels may require scrolling. Its display buttons reveal the wall, guides, voice control, card, bot tiles, tutor, and settings. Turn narration is spoken aloud; its text is no longer shown on screen but remains available to screen readers. The buttons scroll sideways if they do not all fit. Exposed bot melds remain visible; the bot-hands setting still controls whether their concealed tiles are face up. The layout preference is saved in this browser when storage is available.

At the deal and whenever your hand or chosen target changes (including draws and Charleston passes), your tiles automatically arrange into your target's groups, with unused tiles at the end. American card hands follow the target's printed group order, including jokers in eligible groups; other hands follow the tutor's sets-and-pair plan. Your manual order stays in place between those changes.

Use **Undo** to reverse your most recent supported action before another player acts. After you discard or pass a claim, play pauses: choose **Undo** to change your decision, **Continue** after a discard, or **Draw** after passing a claim. Continue or Draw makes the move final. Draws, claims, wins, joker exchanges, Riichi, concealed Kongs, and the optional Charleston choice can also be undone until your next action. Charleston tile passes themselves cannot be undone because all four players pass simultaneously.

## Pattern to win and bot play

Every ruleset shows the pattern you need to win as text in the center of the table. For card-based American hands this is the chosen target's definition (for example "2026: 222 000 2222 6666 · Any 2 suits · 25 pts") with tile images of the suit variation you are closest to (tiles you still need are dimmed), or **Default: any pattern on the card**. For the other rulesets and card-free American practice, it is the default standard hand, such as **Default: 4 sets + pair**; Riichi adds a reminder that the hand also needs riichi or a yaku. Special hands such as seven pairs are not planned.

The bots also play toward their own best estimated pattern. On an American card, each bot compares completion effort: missing tiles plus a scarcity cost based on its own hand, public discards, and exposed groups. Patterns that cannot be filled from the unseen supply rank behind viable ones; jokers can fill sets but never singles or pairs, and the same joker is not counted twice. Opponents' hidden hands and the wall order are not inspected, even when bot tiles are displayed. In other rulesets, each bot aims at its closest sets-and-pair hand while favoring tiles that are still unseen. Bots use this plan to choose Charleston passes, discards, joker exchanges, and concealed kongs. They claim a discard only when it improves their estimated progress. Riichi bots declare riichi when they can, and once a hand is open they claim only pungs that give it a yaku. The tutor's discard and claim advice uses the same planner. A human's chosen American target remains pinned; if the visible supply makes it impossible, the advice warns rather than silently changing it.

In **American**, enter a positive whole number in **Test runs**, then choose **Bot test** (next to New hand) to watch bots play all four seats, including yours. All four use the same bot strategy: at each decision, they re-evaluate every pattern on the active card that fits their exposures, ignoring your human target selection. They can change their preferred pattern automatically as their hands or the visible tile availability change and can win on any active card pattern. Completion effort accounts for both missing tiles and scarcity; this is a heuristic, not an exact win-probability calculation or a guarantee of winning. All four use the same claim, discard, and joker-exchange decisions. Each run plays from the deal and Charleston until a bot wins. If the wall runs out, the next hand starts automatically, with no hand limit. The results show completed runs out of the requested total, total hands played (including the current hand while running), and wins for each bot, including your seat's bot. Results remain visible after completion or stopping; starting a new batch resets them. A stopped, unfinished run does not count as completed, but its hands are included in hands played. Narration is muted and your tiles can't be clicked during the test. Choose **Stop test**, **New hand**, or a different ruleset to stop it; stopping mid-hand hands your seat back to you. Test results count toward the scores.
## American cards and examples

The card controls appear only when the ruleset is **American**, and the selected card pattern is drawn in the center of the American table, scaled to fit both layouts and orientations (on iPhone, choose **Show card**). The ruleset choice is remembered; switching rulesets starts a fresh hand if nothing has been played yet, otherwise it applies to the next hand.

**Card** picks a saved card. The **2026 card (your list)** is built in and selected by default; **Simplified practice (no card)** plays without a card. **Target pattern** chooses what to aim for: **Best for my hand (auto)**, the default, picks the pattern your tiles are most likely to complete when the hand is dealt, rechecks it after every Charleston pass, and says at the end of the Charleston whether it changed. It counts the tiles each pattern still needs, adds a little for tiles that are scarce (copies you can't see, plus jokers for groups of three or more), skips patterns that can no longer be completed, and prefers higher points on a tie. **Default** aims for whichever card pattern your tiles are closest to, and a named pattern makes it your target: the tutor's pass, discard, and claim advice focuses on it, and the center shows it. Either way, every player can win with any pattern on the card, each bot aims for its own closest pattern, and the first player to complete a pattern wins. When a hand ends, the center shows the pattern the winner completed. **Hand type** applies to all four players and takes effect at the next deal (or immediately if the tiles have not been dealt). **Exposed (X)** uses the card's X patterns: players may call discards to complete any group, called groups sit face up on the rack, and opponents may swap matching tiles for jokers in them. **Concealed (C)** uses the card's C patterns: players may call a discard only as their winning 14th tile, all tiles stay hidden, and jokers cannot be swapped out. If the card has no C patterns (as with the built-in 2026 card), the X patterns are played fully concealed and score +10 points. Your card, hand type, and target choices are saved.

Each new card-based American hand, the deal, and the end of the Charleston switch your target to **Best for my hand (auto)**, replacing any saved named target. During normal play your target stays fixed. If another pattern has a strictly lower estimated completion effort, a notice beside your play controls asks **Change target** or **Keep my target**, showing how many tiles each needs. The tutor follows your accepted target, not the unaccepted suggestion. Keeping a target dismisses that suggestion until your hand changes. New hands and completed Charlestons still select automatically; a recheck can keep the same pattern if it remains best.

Passing a discard claim does not grant an extra draw. If the discard came from the player immediately before you and no claims remain, **Draw** appears immediately and **Undo** remains available. Otherwise, **Draw** resolves remaining claims and lets intervening players finish, then automatically draws once on your next legal turn. **Draw queued** shows while you wait. A new claim decision for you cancels the queued draw so you can choose again. A successful claim can skip intervening players' turns; a win or an empty wall ends the hand without your draw.

When tutor guidance is on in a card-based American hand, the tutor names your target and the exact tiles missing, lists other close patterns, and suggests the discard or the three Charleston passes (dashed outline) that give up the least. It also advises whether to continue the Charleston, whether a claim improves completion effort or exposes too early, and when to exchange a natural tile for an exposed joker. Availability-aware discard and claim advice accounts for public discards and exposures, but does not inspect hidden hands or predict other players' choices.

Open **Edit, copy, import, or export cards** to change cards. Edit the name or patterns and choose **Save changes**, or **Save as new card** to keep the original. **Restore original 2026 card** resets the built-in card; **Delete card** removes a card you added. **Export** downloads the selected card as a text file and **Import** loads one as a new card, which is useful for backups or moving cards to another device. Each valid pattern is previewed as tiles with its number of variations. Saving checks every line and reports the first problem.

Write one pattern per line as `name | points | X or C | regular or singles | groups | suit rule`; the name and suit rule are optional, and lines starting with `#` are comments. Groups are `tile:count`, totaling 14 tiles. Tiles are `C1`–`C9` (Craks), `D1`–`D9` (Dots), `B1`–`B9` (Bams), winds `E S W N`, dragons `R G 0` (0 is the white dragon/soap), and `F` (flower). Suit letters `x y z v` stand for different suits (`x2:3` is three 2s in one suit), `xn` through `xn+8` make a consecutive run in any starting number, `Dx` is the dragon matching suit `x`, `D*` is any dragon, and `W*` is any wind. Without a suit rule, different letters must be different suits (four letters use all three suits); a suit rule such as `suits 2` or `suits 1-3` sets how many suits a pattern uses instead. Each pattern expands into every allowed variation.

The 2026 card is based on the user's supplied list, not an official card. Patterns from that list whose tiles do not total 14, or that use unclear `1D` notation, are kept as `#` comments to fix in the editor. The consecutive pattern labeled as a quint is implemented exactly as written, with four 1s and four 5s. On the built-in card the winner gains the listed points and other players lose none; on your own cards, each other player pays the listed value (a discarder or every player on a self-draw pays double, and jokerless non-singles hands double again). Scores accumulate across hands until reload.

Cards are stored in this browser's local storage, not sent to a server. They are not shared across browsers or devices and may be removed when browser data is cleared, so export cards you want to keep. Saving edits affects the next hand, or the current one if the tiles have not been dealt yet. Storage errors are displayed in the editor.

Private-card wins use the entered value and apply discard, self-draw, jokerless, and Singles and Pairs payout multipliers. Scores are tracked separately from the other modes. Dead-hand declarations, courtesy passes, and special penalty rules are not implemented. Card-based discard review is also not implemented.

## Publish with GitHub Pages

1. Sign in to GitHub from VS Code using the **Accounts** menu.
2. In **Source Control** (`Ctrl+Shift+G`), commit the staged files. If Git asks for your identity, configure it locally:

   ```powershell
   git config --local user.name "Your Name"
   git config --local user.email "your-github-noreply-address"
   ```

3. Choose **Publish to GitHub** or **Publish Branch**, name the repository `mahjong-game`, and select **Private**.
4. In the new repository, open **Settings → Pages** and set the publishing source to **GitHub Actions**.
5. After the workflow succeeds, get the site URL from the deployment in **Actions** or from **Settings → Pages**. Future pushes to `main` deploy automatically.

The source repository can be private while the published site is public. GitHub requires an Enterprise Cloud organization to publish a private Pages site. Do not put confidential content in the game files.

To try the game locally, serve this folder with a static web server and open `index.html`.
